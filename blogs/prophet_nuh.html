🚢 Prophet Nuh (Noah) ﷺ

The longest-serving messenger in history — 950 years of calling to Allah, building an ark on dry land, and witnessing the flood that cleansed the earth.

📖 “Indeed, We sent Nuh to his people, [saying]: 'Warn your people before there comes to them a painful punishment.'” — Quran 71:1

Among the great prophets of Allah, Nuh (Noah, peace be upon him) holds a unique distinction: he spent 950 years calling his people to monotheism. He is often called the “second father of humanity” because all surviving nations after the flood descended from his offspring. His story is not merely about a ship and a storm — it is about unwavering patience, the tragedy of rejecting truth, and the mercy of Allah that saves the believers. It is a warning, a comfort, and a timeless lesson for every generation.

✨ The context of idolatry: “And [the idolaters] said: 'Never leave your gods and never leave Wadd, nor Suwa' or Yaghooth and Ya'ooq and Nasr [i.e., the names of their idols].'”
— Surah Nuh (71:23)

🌄 The World Before Nuh: The First Idolatry

Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) informed us that the period between Adam and Nuh was ten centuries. During those generations, people were upon righteousness. Then, after the death of five righteous men — Wadd, Suwa', Yaghooth, Ya'ooq, and Nasr — Satan whispered to their people to erect statues in their memory. Over time, the purpose was forgotten, and the statues were worshipped as gods. This was the origin of idolatry on earth. The people believed these idols could bring rain, protect them from harm, and intercede with Allah. They had strayed far from the pure monotheism taught by Adam.

Into this darkness, Allah sent Nuh (ﷺ) — a man of eloquence, patience, and profound sincerity. He was the first messenger sent to a people who had fallen into widespread shirk (associating partners with Allah).

🗣️ The Call: Night and Day, Secretly and Openly

Nuh (ﷺ) began his mission with gentle wisdom. He pointed to the signs of Allah: the alternation of night and day, the sun, the moon, the stars, the rain that brings life to dead earth. He asked: “What is [the matter] with you that you do not attribute to Allah [due] grandeur, while He has created you in stages?” (71:13-14). He urged them to worship Allah alone, to fear Him, and to obey him as a trustworthy messenger.

He called them night and day, but his invitation only increased their aversion. They put their fingers in their ears, covered themselves with garments, persisted in arrogance, and mocked him. The rich and powerful elites rejected him, saying: “We do not see you but as a man like ourselves, and we do not see you followed except by those who are the lowest of us [and] at first suggestion. And we do not see in you over us any merit; rather, we think you are a liar.” (11:27).

Nuh responded with dignity: “I ask not of you for it any wealth. My reward is not but from Allah. And I am not one to drive away those who have believed.” (11:29). He refused to dismiss the poor believers to please the wealthy disbelievers — a principle that forever established that faith is not measured by social status.

💡 Profound lesson: The elites of Nuh’s people rejected him not because of intellectual weakness, but because of arrogance and attachment to power. They feared that if they accepted the truth, they would lose their status. This same pattern repeats in every generation.

⏳ 950 Years of Steadfastness

The Quran tells us: “And We certainly sent Nuh to his people, and he remained among them a thousand years minus fifty years…” (29:14). For nine and a half centuries, Nuh continued to preach. Generation after generation, fathers advised their sons to reject Nuh. The number of believers remained small — a few poor, weak, and sincere souls. Yet Nuh never despaired. He addressed them publicly and privately, sometimes in gatherings, sometimes in secret counsel. He reminded them of Allah’s forgiveness: “Ask forgiveness of your Lord. Indeed, He is ever a Perpetual Forgiver. He will send [rain from] the sky upon you in [continuing] showers. And give you increase in wealth and children and provide for you gardens and provide for you rivers.” (71:10-12).

But the people only grew more obstinate. They challenged him: “Bring us what you threaten us with, if you should be of the truthful.” (11:32). Nuh replied that only Allah could bring punishment, and he could not benefit them if Allah had willed their misguidance.

🛠️ Building the Ark: Mockery Turns to Astonishment

Finally, Allah revealed to Nuh that no more would believe. He was commanded to build an ark under divine supervision. He chose a spot far from the sea and began gathering wood and tools. As he worked, the disbelievers passed by and mocked him: “O Nuh! Does carpentry appeal to you more than prophethood? Why are you building an ark so far from the sea? Are you going to drag it to the water?” Nuh replied: “If you ridicule us, then we will ridicule you just as you ridicule. And you are going to know who will get a punishment that will disgrace him [on earth] and upon whom will descend an enduring punishment [in the Hereafter].'” (11:38-39).

The ark was built of planks and nails, a massive vessel that seemed absurd to a people who had never seen such a thing. But Nuh trusted Allah completely.

📖 The divine command: “And construct the ship under Our Eyes and Our inspiration and do not address Me concerning those who have wronged; indeed, they are [to be] drowned.”
— Surah Hud (11:37)

💧 The Flood: The Oven Overflows

Allah gave Nuh a sign: when water gushed forth from the tannur (oven) at his house, the time had come. Nuh boarded the ark with the believers — a small group — and took with him pairs of every species: male and female, of animals, birds, and insects. His wife and one of his sons, who had pretended faith, refused to board.

Then the heavens opened. Rain poured in torrents never seen before or since. Water burst from the earth from every crack. The waves rose like mountains. The ark sailed, carrying its precious cargo of believers and creatures. Nuh saw his son struggling to climb a high mountain, calling out: “O my son! Come aboard with us and be not with the disbelievers.” The son replied arrogantly: “I will take refuge on a mountain to protect me from the water.” Nuh cried: “There is no protector today from the decree of Allah, except for whom He gives mercy.” And the waves came between them, and his son was among the drowned (11:42-43).

Every disbeliever on earth perished — the flood was not local but universal, cleansing the earth of the corruption of idolatry. The ark came to rest on Mount Judi, and it was said: “O Earth! Swallow up your water, and O sky! Withhold your rain.” The water subsided, and the matter was accomplished (11:44).

😢 Nuh’s Grief and Allah’s Gentle Correction

Nuh (ﷺ), in his human love, called out to Allah: “My Lord! Indeed my son is of my family; and indeed, Your promise is true; and You are the most just of judges!” Allah replied with a gentle but firm correction: “O Nuh! Indeed he is not of your family; indeed, he is [one whose] work was other than righteous, so ask Me not for that about which you have no knowledge. Indeed, I advise you, lest you be among the ignorant.” (11:45-46). Nuh immediately repented: “My Lord! I seek refuge in You from asking that of which I have no knowledge. And unless You forgive me and have Mercy upon me, I will be among the losers.” (11:47). This teaches that even prophets are corrected by Allah, and that true family is defined by faith, not blood.

🌟 Reflection: The story of Nuh’s son is heartbreaking. A mountain could not save him from Allah’s decree. It reminds us that no material power — wealth, status, or strength — can protect anyone from divine punishment. Salvation is only through faith and obedience.

🌱 The New Beginning: Humanity Renewed

After the flood, Nuh and the believers disembarked. They kindled a fire — for fire had been forbidden on the ark — and ate hot food for the first time in months. They fasted a day in gratitude to Allah. The ark had settled, and a new chapter of human history began. All of humanity after the flood descended from Nuh’s three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth. Nuh lived on for many years, and on his deathbed, he advised his children with two commands and two prohibitions.

The Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) narrated: “When the death of Nuh approached, he admonished his sons: 'I command you to believe that there is no deity except Allah, and that if the seven heavens and the seven earths were put on one side of a scale and the words 'There is no deity but Allah' were put on the other, the latter would outweigh the former. I warn you against associating partners with Allah and against pride.'” (Bukhari).

📚 Major Lessons from the Story of Nuh (PBUH)

  • Patience in the face of rejection: Nuh called for 950 years with little to show. His perseverance is a model for every caller to truth.
  • The danger of idolatry: Idolatry begins with revering righteous people, then gradually turns into worship. Islam prohibits anything that leads to shirk, including graves and statues.
  • Faith is not inherited: Nuh’s own son and wife perished because they did not believe. Blood relation does not guarantee salvation.
  • Mockery does not change truth: The disbelievers laughed at the ark, but their laughter turned to terror when the flood came.
  • Allah’s mercy precedes His wrath: The flood came only after centuries of warning. Allah gives ample time for repentance.
  • Pride is the root of destruction: The disbelievers were arrogant, refusing to follow a “poor” messenger. Pride drowned them.
  • True wealth is faith: Nuh did not chase the rich; he embraced the sincere poor. In Allah’s sight, the believers are the elite.
🌊 A universal sign: The story of Nuh is preserved not only in the Quran but in the traditions of many cultures worldwide. It is a reminder that destruction follows when a society abandons the worship of its Creator. Yet, for those who believe, the ark is always ready.

🕊️ Nuh’s Legacy: The Second Father of Mankind

Nuh (ﷺ) is one of the five greatest prophets (Ulul ‘Azm). He is praised in the Quran as “a grateful servant” (17:3). His ark became a sign: “Indeed, when the water overflowed, We carried you in the sailing ship. That We might make it for you a reminder and [that] a listening ear would heed it.” (69:11-12). Every time a Muslim reads Surah Nuh or the passages in Surah Hud, they are reminded that patience and trust in Allah always triumph — even when the whole world seems to mock you.

May Allah make us of those who heed the warning, who board the ark of obedience, and who never despair of Allah’s mercy. The flood of punishment is real, but so is the ship of salvation — following the prophets and their teachings.

🕋 And indeed, Nuh called upon Us, and We are the best of responders. And We saved him and his family from the great affliction. (Quran 37:75-76)

O Allah, grant us the patience of Nuh, the gratitude of Nuh, and the certainty that Your promise is true. Do not make us among those who mock the truth or rely on worldly mountains. Save us by Your mercy and make us of the righteous who board the ship of Islam. Ameen.

“And it sailed with them through waves like mountains…” — A verse that echoes through time, reminding every soul that the ark of Allah’s mercy is always open, but the door closes when the command comes.
