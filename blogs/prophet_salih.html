🐪 Prophet Salih (Saleh) ﷺ

The messenger to Thamud — a people who carved palaces from mountains, demanded a miracle, then killed the very sign sent to them. Their end was a single cry and an earthquake that silenced them forever.

📖 “And to Thamud [We sent] their brother Salih. He said, 'O my people, worship Allah; you have no deity other than Him...'” — Quran 11:61

After the destruction of the people of 'Aad, the tribe of Thamud rose to power and prosperity. They were master builders, carving magnificent homes into mountains and erecting lofty palaces on the plains. But material wealth brought spiritual decay. They fell into idolatry, tyranny, and oppression. Into this society of arrogance and power, Allah sent Salih (peace be upon him), a man from among them known for his wisdom, purity, and noble character. His story is a stark warning against rejecting clear signs, conspiring against truth, and the fatal consequences of arrogance.

✨ The call to monotheism: “And to Thamood [We sent] their brother Saalih. He said, 'O my people, worship Allah; you have no deity other than Him. There has come to you clear evidence from your Lord. This is the she-camel of Allah [sent] to you as a sign. So leave her to eat within Allah's land and do not touch her with harm, lest there seize you a painful punishment.'”
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:73)

🏔️ The People of Thamud: Builders of Mountains

Thamud succeeded the people of 'Aad in power. They were given fertile land, strong bodies, and incredible engineering skills. They carved spacious homes into mountains, believing their strength would protect them forever. But they forgot the One who gave them that strength. They worshipped idols, spread corruption, and the rich and powerful oppressed the weak. When Salih (ﷺ) called them to worship Allah alone, they were initially respectful because of his noble standing among them. The Quran records them saying: “O Salih, you were among us a man of promise before this. Do you forbid us to worship what our fathers worshipped? And indeed we are, about that to which you invite us, in disquieting doubt.” (11:62). They clung to the traditions of their forefathers without reason or proof.

🐫 The Miracle: A She-Camel from the Rock

To convince them, the people demanded a miracle. They pointed to a specific rock and challenged Salih: “Ask your Lord to bring forth a she-camel, ten months pregnant, tall and unique, from this rock.” Salih took a solemn vow from them that if Allah granted this sign, they would believe. He prayed to Allah, and the rock split open. From it emerged a magnificent she-camel, heavily pregnant, unlike any seen before. It was a clear, undeniable sign — a living miracle. The Quran says: “And We gave Thamud the she-camel as a visible sign, but they wronged her.” (17:59).

The she-camel was not an ordinary animal. It drank most of the water from the wells on one day, and on alternate days it gave enough milk to satisfy all the people. No other animals could approach when it drank. It grazed freely on Allah’s earth. Many believed in Salih after witnessing this miracle, but the arrogant elite remained stubborn.

💡 Profound lesson: The she-camel was a living, breathing miracle — a gift and a test. Allah could have destroyed them immediately, but He gave them a chance. Their treatment of this blessed beast would determine their fate. It was a sign that demanded gratitude, but they responded with hatred.

🗡️ The Conspiracy: Killing the Sign

The disbelievers could not bear the she-camel. They complained that it frightened their livestock and consumed too much water. But the real reason was pride: they refused to accept that a miraculous beast proved Salih’s prophethood. A conspiracy was hatched. Two women from noble families — Saduq bint Mahya and Aneezah — tempted young men with offers of marriage and wealth to hamstring the she-camel. Masrai ibn Mahraj and Qudar ibn Saluf, along with seven others, formed a gang of nine ringleaders.

They watched the she-camel as it came to drink. Masrai shot it in the leg with an arrow. When it tried to flee, Qudar struck it with a sword and hamstrung it. The pregnant she-camel fell to the ground, and they killed it. Then they celebrated, composing poetry in praise of the murder. They even plotted to kill Salih and his family that night, swearing by Allah that they would deny involvement.

Salih warned them: “Enjoy yourselves in your homes for three days. That is a promise not to be denied [i.e., unfailing].” (11:65). He pleaded with them: “O my people, why are you impatient for evil before [i.e., instead of] good? Why do you not seek forgiveness of Allah that you may receive mercy?” (27:46). They arrogantly replied: “We consider you a bad omen, you and those with you.” (27:47).

📖 The nine ringleaders: “And there were in the city nine family heads causing corruption in the land and not amending [its affairs]. They said, 'Take a mutual oath by Allah that we will kill him by night, he and his family. Then we will say to his executor, "We did not witness the destruction of his family, and indeed, we are truthful."' And they planned a plan, and We planned a plan, while they perceived not.”
— Surah An-Naml (27:48-50)

💥 The Destruction: A Cry and an Earthquake

Salih, knowing that the punishment was imminent, left the city with the believers. He turned away from the wrongdoers. On the appointed day — the third day after the warning — the sky turned red, then dark. A terrifying cry (sayhah) filled the air, followed by a violent earthquake that shook the entire land. The mighty mountains, the carved palaces, the strong bodies — none could protect them. The Quran describes: “So the earthquake seized them, and they became within their home [corpses] fallen prone.” (7:78). Another verse says: “Then look how was the outcome of their plan - that We destroyed them and their people, all. So those are their houses, desolate because of the wrong they had done. Indeed in that is a sign for people who know.” (27:51-52).

They were struck dead in an instant — no warning, no escape. Their magnificent homes lay empty, a silent monument to divine wrath. The believers were saved because they had left with Salih.

🌟 Reflection: The destruction of Thamud was not random. They were given a clear miracle, yet they killed it. They plotted to kill the prophet, yet they thought they could outsmart Allah. Their end was sudden, humiliating, and total. The same earth that they carved and dominated swallowed their pride and their lives.

🕋 A Lesson for the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) and His Companions

On the way to the Battle of Tabuk, the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) and his companions passed by the ruins of Thamud — the very houses carved into the mountains. Some companions began to draw water from the wells that the people of Thamud had used. The Prophet (ﷺ) ordered them to pour out the water and not to drink from it, nor to use the dough they had made from that water. He said: “I fear that you may be affected by what afflicted them; so do not enter upon them.” (Bukhari). He covered his face and hastened past the valley. This teaches us to take lessons from the punishment of past nations and not to treat their ruins as tourist attractions without reflection and fear.

📚 Major Lessons from the Story of Salih (PBUH)

  • Miracles do not guarantee belief: The people of Thamud saw a living, impossible miracle — a pregnant she-camel emerging from solid rock. Yet most remained disbelievers. The heart’s arrogance is the greatest barrier.
  • The danger of blind tradition: They said, “Do you forbid us to worship what our fathers worshipped?” They followed their ancestors without evidence. Truth is not determined by majority or heritage.
  • Conspiracy against truth always fails: The nine ringleaders plotted to kill Salih and his family. But Allah planned, and His plan is the best. They were destroyed, and the believers were saved.
  • Material strength is no protection: The Thamud were masters of architecture and strength. Their mountains did not save them. Only faith and obedience to Allah protect.
  • Do not dwell in the lands of the punished with heedlessness: The Prophet (ﷺ) warned against entering such places without humility and reflection. Take lessons, not pride.
  • The punishment was swift and decisive: A single cry and an earthquake. There was no prolonged war or suffering — just the decree of Allah, executed in an instant.
🐪 The she-camel as a test: The she-camel was a mercy and a test. When they killed it, they killed their own chance of salvation. In Islamic tradition, harming or killing an animal without right is a sin, but this was a direct violation of Allah’s command. The punishment was proportional: they rejected the sign, so the sign was taken away, and they were destroyed.

🌍 The Legacy of Thamud: A Sign for All Time

The ruins of Thamud — known as Al-Hijr or Mada'in Salih — still exist in northwestern Saudi Arabia. They stand as a silent witness to the truth of Allah’s words. The Quran says: “And certainly did the companions of al-Hijr [i.e., Thamud] deny the messengers.” (15:80). Every traveler who passes by those carved mountains is reminded that no strength, no wealth, no ingenuity can stand against the will of Allah. The people of Thamud were given every chance. They had a prophet from among them, a clear miracle, and a warning. They chose arrogance and paid the price.

Salih (ﷺ) turned away from them with these words: “O my people, I had certainly conveyed to you the message of my Lord and advised you, but you do not like advisors.” (7:79). His mission was complete. The rest was with Allah.

🏜️ So those are their houses, desolate because of the wrong they had done. Indeed in that is a sign for people who know. (Quran 27:52)

O Allah, protect us from the arrogance of Thamud. Do not make us of those who reject clear signs out of pride. Grant us hearts that submit, eyes that see the truth, and feet that walk away from destruction. Make us of the believers who were saved, not of the plotters who were destroyed. Ameen.

📚 Based on Surah Al-A'raf (7), Surah Hud (11), Surah An-Naml (27), Surah Al-Hijr (15), and Sahih al-Bukhari. May Allah make us of those who take heed.
