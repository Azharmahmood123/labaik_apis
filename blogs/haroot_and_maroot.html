📖 The Story of Haaroot & Maaroot

A trial from the heavens · Magic · Faith & protection in Islam

Among the profound narratives in the Quran is the story of two angels, Haaroot and Maaroot, sent down to the city of Babylon as a divine test for humanity. Allah, the All‑Wise, reveals in Surah Al‑Baqarah (2:102) the reality behind magic, the slander against Prophet Sulaymaan (Solomon), and how the Children of Israel turned away from the truth, indulging in what harmed them instead of the Book of Allah.

📜 Quran 2:102 (interpretation of the meaning):
“And they followed [instead] what the devils had recited during the reign of Sulaymaan. It was not Sulaymaan who disbelieved, but the devils disbelieved, teaching people magic and that which was revealed to the two angels at Babylon, Haaroot and Maaroot. But they [the two angels] do not teach anyone unless they say: 'We are a trial, so do not disbelieve [by practicing magic].' And yet they learn from them that by which they cause separation between a man and his wife. But they do not harm anyone through it except by permission of Allah...”

🔹 Turning Away From the Book of Allah

The Children of Israel had been given the Tawraah (Torah), yet a faction among them disregarded it, casting the Scripture behind their backs as if they knew nothing. As a consequence, Allah busied them with futile matters and plunged them into misguidance. Whoever shuns the worship of the Most Merciful becomes afflicted with idol worship; whoever shuns the truth will be left with falsehood. The Jews during and after the reign of Sulaymaan (peace be upon him) refused to believe, and thus followed the fabrications of the devils who falsely claimed that Sulaymaan ruled by magic.

💡 Divine justice: When a people abandon what benefits them — divine guidance, monotheism, the Sunnah — they become trapped in innovations, blind imitation, and the traps of Shaytan. The story of Haaroot and Maaroot is a living lesson about trial, free will, and the grave consequences of sorcery.

🔹 The Two Angels: A Test From Allah

Allah sent Haaroot and Maaroot not to promote disbelief, but as a means of tribulation. Before teaching any form of magic, the angels would firmly warn: “We are a trial, so do not disbelieve.” Only those who insisted on learning, despite the warning, were taught. Their knowledge included what could separate a husband and wife — but such harm only occurs by the will of Allah, and the magician chooses disbelief by practicing it. Magic is a reality, not mere illusion, and its effects are severe, yet it remains under the ultimate decree of Allah.

⚡ The Reality of Magic & Its Evil Consequences

Allah describes marriage as a bond of affection and mercy (Quran 30:21), yet magic can shatter that bond, turning love into hatred, or causing a person to imagine repulsive things about their spouse. Magicians may bewitch the eyes — as the sorcerers of Pharaoh did, making ropes appear as snakes — or create discord between loved ones. The Prophet (ﷺ) said: “Satan places his throne on water, then sends his troops… the closest to him is the one who causes the most mischief, especially separating a man from his wife.” (Muslim).

🌙 Prophetic warning: “The punishment for the magician is that he must be beheaded.” (At‑Tirmidhi & Al‑Haakim). Magic constitutes apostasy, nullifying one’s deeds even if they pray or fast.

📿 The Correct Way to Seek Cure From Magic

It is strictly forbidden to approach fortune‑tellers or magicians to remove spells. The Prophet (ﷺ) said: “He who approaches a magician and believes what he says will have his prayers rejected for forty days.” (Muslim). Instead, the legislated Ruqyah (Quranic recitation and authentic supplications) is the remedy. Surah Al‑Faatihah, Ayat‑ul‑Kursi (2:255), Al‑Ikhlas, Al‑Falaq, and An‑Naas are powerful means. The following prophetic du‘a is also highly recommended:

“Allaahumma Rabban‑naas, athhibil‑ba’s, washfi, Antash‑Shaafi, laa shifaa’a illaa shifaa’uka, shifaa’an laa yughaadiru saqama.”
(O Allah, Lord of mankind, remove the harm and cure. You are the Curer. There is no cure except Your cure, a cure that leaves no disease.) – Al‑Bukhari

Some of the righteous predecessors (such as Wahb ibn Munabbih) recommended reciting Quranic verses addressing magic (from Surah Al‑A‘raaf, Yoonus, Taa Haa) over water, mixing crushed lote‑tree leaves, and bathing with it — a method known to be beneficial by experience, while always relying on Allah alone.

🌟 The Prophet (ﷺ) & The Incident of Magic

‘Aa’ishah (may Allah be pleased with her) narrated that a Jew named Labeed ibn Al‑A‘sam bewitched the Prophet (ﷺ) so that he would imagine doing things he had not done. Jibreel informed him of the location of the talisman — inside a well called Tharwaan, under a rock, inside a pollen sheath with a comb and knotted hair. The Prophet (ﷺ) recovered through Allah’s power and did not execute the magician publicly, fearing that people might then accuse innocent believers without clear evidence. This shows his immense mercy and wisdom in preserving justice.

Some might ask: “How could a prophet be bewitched?” The answer is that the magic only affected worldly matters — specifically his relations with his wives — and never touched his mind, memory, or the revelation. Allah protected him from being prevented from conveying the message, as promised in Surah Al‑Ma’idah (5:67), yet he was tested with harm from disbelievers, hunger, and even a pit, but his mission remained intact.

⚖️ Rulings, Misconceptions & Final Warnings

Any form of magic — even if claimed to be for “reconciliation” or “love” — is haram and constitutes disbelief. Ends never justify evil means. Wearing amulets for protection against magic is a form of shirk (associating partners with Allah) and only weakens one’s faith. Moreover, some fabricated narrations like “Learn magic but do not teach it” are lies attributed to the Prophet (ﷺ).

🕋 Important: The most skilled magicians historically were often from among those who abandoned Allah’s scripture; the Jews became known for sorcery after shunning the prophethood of Muhammad (ﷺ). Magic causes separation, oppression, and sometimes even death. A magician who kills by sorcery is to be executed as a murderer.

📖 Lessons From the Story of Haaroot & Maaroot

  • ✔ Magic is a reality, not mere illusion, and practicing it is an act of disbelief.
  • ✔ Allah tests His servants with trials — the angels themselves were a test to distinguish the believers from the wicked.
  • ✔ Turning away from the Book of Allah leads to falling into satanic traps and destructive habits.
  • ✔ The only protection and cure lie in the Quran, prophetic supplications, and sincere reliance on Allah, not in counter‑magic.
  • ✔ Spouses must safeguard their marriage with faith, prayer, and ruqyah, especially because shaytan works hardest to separate husband and wife.
  • ✔ A Muslim never approaches a magician, fortune‑teller, or astrologer — doing so nullifies one’s prayers for forty days and diminishes faith.
🌿 Quran 2:102 (continued): “But they [the Children of Israel] certainly knew that whoever purchased it [magic] would not have in the Hereafter any share. And wretched is that for which they sold themselves, if they only knew.”

The story of Haaroot and Maaroot echoes through time as a sobering reminder. Magic may appear impressive, but its spiritual price is eternal loss. Instead, believers hold firmly to the Quran, the Sunnah, and the remembrance of Allah, seeking refuge in Him from the whisperers and from the trials of this world. May Allah protect us from every harm and grant us steadfastness upon the truth.
