👑 Prophet Sulayman (Solomon) ﷺ

The king, prophet, and judge who spoke with birds, commanded the jinn, and subdued the wind — a kingdom like none before or after.

Qur'anic epic | Lessons of humility & power

Among the greatest messengers and rulers ever to walk the earth, Sulayman ibn Dawood (Solomon, peace be upon him) stands as a unique figure. His story, intricately woven into the Quran, transcends ordinary kingship — he was granted miracles that testified to Allah’s absolute sovereignty. From understanding the language of birds to mobilizing armies of jinn and men, his life radiates signs for those who reflect.

📜 Inherited Wisdom, Not Wealth

Sulayman (ﷺ) inherited his prophethood and dominion from his father, Prophet Dawood (David, PBUH). However, as Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) clarified: “We (Prophets) do not inherit anything, and whatever we leave is to be used for charity.” (Al-Bukhari). Thus, Sulayman’s inheritance was not material — it was divine knowledge, justice, and the responsibility to guide people. The Quran states: “And We had certainly given to Dawood and Sulayman knowledge, and they said: 'Praise [is due] to Allah, who has favored us over many of His believing servants.'” (27:15).

📖 The Unique Bounty: “And gathered for Sulayman were his soldiers of the jinn and men and birds, and they were [marching] in rows. Until, when they came upon the valley of the ants, an ant said: 'O ants! Enter your dwellings that you not be crushed by Sulayman and his soldiers while they perceive not.' So [Sulayman] smiled, amused at her speech, and said: 'My Lord! Enable me to be grateful for Your favour...'”
— Surah An-Naml (27:17-19)

🐎 The Lesson of the Racehorses

One afternoon, Sulayman was reviewing his squadron of noble, poised horses. So captivated was he by their beauty and strength that he continued admiring them until the sun had nearly set and the time for the mid-afternoon prayer ('Asr) had passed. Immediately regretful, he exclaimed: “Indeed I gave preference to the love of good [things] over the remembrance of my Lord until it disappeared into the curtain of darkness.” (Quran 38:32). He ordered the horses to be brought back and struck their legs and necks — not out of cruelty, but as a sacrifice for Allah, showing that nothing, not even prized possessions, should distract from the Creator’s remembrance.

💡 Reflection: This episode teaches us that even the most powerful prophet was tested with worldly attachments. True repentance involves decisive action to refocus on Allah. The love of horses (or any worldly good) must never overshadow divine duty.

🌬️ The Miraculous Kingdom: Wind, Copper, and Jinn

Sulayman (ﷺ) begged Allah for a kingdom that none after him would possess — and his prayer was answered. The wind was made subservient to him: its morning journey covered a month’s distance, and the evening journey another month. A spring of molten copper flowed for him. The jinn worked under his command — building lofty halls, statues, huge basins like reservoirs, and fixed cauldrons. Yet Allah reminded them: “Work, O family of Dawood, in gratitude.” (34:13). But few of Allah’s servants are truly grateful.

🐜 The Valley of the Ants — Compassion in Leadership

While marching with his massive army (comprising men, jinn, and birds) through a valley, Sulayman heard an ant warning her colony: “Enter your dwellings that you not be crushed by Sulayman and his soldiers while they perceive not.” He smiled, grateful that the ant knew his prophetic nature — that he would never intentionally harm Allah’s creatures. He immediately prayed for the ability to remain thankful and to do righteous deeds, seeking Allah’s mercy to enter among the righteous. This scene underlines the believer’s gentleness and attentiveness to even the smallest of Allah’s creation.

👸 The Queen of Sheba (Bilqis) — A Journey to Monotheism

One day, Sulayman inspected the hoopoe bird and found it missing. When it returned, it brought astonishing news: the land of Sheba (Saba’) was ruled by a powerful queen who possessed a magnificent throne, yet she and her people worshipped the sun instead of Allah. Sulayman sent a letter to the queen via the hoopoe: “In the Name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful. Be not haughty with me but come to me in submission [as Muslims].” (27:30-31).

The queen, wise and diplomatic, sent lavish gifts to test Sulayman’s intentions. He rejected the bribery outright, declaring that Allah had given him far superior wealth and that his only mission was to call them to Tawheed (the Oneness of Allah). Realizing the futility of material gifts, the queen decided to visit him herself.

Before her arrival, Sulayman asked his assembly: “Which of you can bring me her throne before they come to me in submission?” A powerful jinn offered to bring it in the blink of an eye, but one who had knowledge of the Scripture said: “I will bring it to you before your glance returns to you.” And instantly, the throne of Bilqis stood before Sulayman. When she arrived, the throne was disguised; she was asked: “Is your throne like this?” She replied: “It is as if it were the very one.” Her intelligence was evident.

Sulayman then invited her into a palace with a floor of crystal-clear glass, shimmering like water. Thinking it was water, she lifted her skirt. He clarified: “Indeed, it is a smooth courtyard of glass.” At that moment, the queen’s heart opened; she declared: “My Lord, indeed I have wronged myself, and I submit with Sulayman to Allah, the Lord of the worlds.” (27:44). Thus, the light of Islam replaced the worship of the sun.

⚰️ The Death of Sulayman — A Final, Profound Lesson

Allah had subjected the jinn to Sulayman, but they were arrogant and spread false beliefs that they knew the unseen. To correct this forever, Sulayman’s death itself became a miracle. He died while sitting erect, leaning on his staff, overseeing the jinn working on a construction site. The jinn continued their toil for a long time, unaware of his death, because his body remained propped up. Only when a termite (a creature of the earth) ate through his staff did his body fall. People realized he had died long ago, and it became clear that the jinn had no knowledge of the unseen — otherwise, they would not have continued in humiliating labor.

Allah revealed: “And when We decreed for him death, nothing indicated to them his death except a creature of the earth eating his staff. But when he fell, it became clear to the jinn that if they had known the unseen, they would not have remained in humiliating punishment.” (Quran 34:14). This powerful conclusion shattered superstition forever: only Allah knows the unseen.

📘 Key Takeaway: Sulayman’s life was an exhibition of divine power, yet his death was a lesson in humility and the absolute Oneness of Allah’s knowledge. Neither prophets nor jinn possess knowledge of the future. Worship, reliance, and fear belong to Allah alone.

🏛️ The Legacy of Sulayman (PBUH)

Throughout history, the name of Sulayman has symbolized wisdom, justice, and authority tempered with devotion. He built the sacred temple in Jerusalem (later known as the Dome of the Rock foundation) and inspired generations to turn to Allah. He demonstrated that power is a trust — not for oppression but for spreading goodness, gratitude, and Tawheed. Despite commanding armies of jinn and birds, he never forgot his own frailty before Allah. His famous supplication: “My Lord! Forgive me and grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me. Indeed, You are the Bestower.” (38:35) — a prayer that was granted, yet he remained a humble servant.

🕌 And in their stories is a lesson for those of understanding. (Quran 12:111)
The tale of Prophet Sulayman (ﷺ) reminds us that every blessing, every empire, every miracle is from Allah. True sovereignty lies in surrendering to Him. May we learn gratitude, shun arrogance, and never claim knowledge of the unseen.
