⚖️ Prophet Shu'ayb (Jethro) ﷺ

The preacher of justice, honest weights, and fair measures — sent to a nation of cheaters and highway robbers. Their punishment: a scorching blast and an earthquake that left them fallen prone.

📖 “Give full measure and do not be of those who cause loss. And weigh with an even balance. And do not deprive people of their due.” — Quran 26:181-183

Among the prophets sent to the Arabian Peninsula, Shu'ayb (Jethro, peace be upon him) stands as the champion of economic justice. He was sent to the people of Madyan (Midian), a prosperous Arab tribe living in the region of Ma'an (modern-day Jordan). They were masters of trade and commerce, but their greed had corrupted them entirely. They cheated in measurements, falsified weights, hid defects in their goods, and even robbed travelers on the roads. Into this morally bankrupt society, Allah sent Shu'ayb — a man known for eloquence, wisdom, and unwavering commitment to truth. His story is a timeless warning against fraud, corruption, and the arrogance of the wealthy.

✨ The call to justice: “And to [the people of] Madyan [We sent] their brother Shu'ayb. He said, 'O my people, worship Allah; you have no deity other than Him. There has come to you clear evidence from your Lord. So fulfill the measure and weight and do not deprive people of their due and cause not corruption upon the earth after its reformation. That is better for you, if you should be believers.'”
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:85)

🏜️ The People of Madyan: A Society of Cheats and Thieves

The people of Madyan were descendants of Prophet Ibrahim (Abraham) through his son Midian. They lived in a lush, fertile valley and engaged in extensive trade. However, their prosperity led to moral decay. They had three major crimes:

  • Cheating in weights and measures: They would take full measure when receiving goods but give less when giving out. They used false scales to defraud customers.
  • Highway robbery: They would sit on roads and threaten travelers, often robbing them or forcing them to pay tribute.
  • Turning people away from Allah: They would mock believers and try to prevent others from following the truth.

Allah had blessed them with abundant resources, yet they used those blessings to commit corruption. They worshiped idols, denied the Hereafter, and oppressed the weak. Into this environment, Shu'ayb (ﷺ) was sent as a messenger from among them.

🗣️ Shu'ayb's Preaching: Honest Weights, Pure Faith

Shu'ayb (ﷺ) first called his people to the foundation of all faith: “Worship Allah; you have no deity other than Him.” Then he addressed their specific sins. He commanded them to give full measure and just weight, not to deprive people of their due, and not to spread corruption on earth. He reminded them of Allah's favors: “And remember when you were few and He increased you.” He warned them about the fate of previous nations: “And see how the end of the corrupters was.” (7:85-86).

He also confronted their practice of threatening believers: “And do not sit on every path, threatening and averting from the way of Allah those who believe in Him, seeking to make it [seem] deviant.” (7:86). Shu'ayb was not asking for any personal reward; he only sought reformation for his people.

💡 Profound lesson: Shu'ayb's message shows that faith in Allah is inseparable from social and economic justice. A believer cannot cheat in business and claim to love Allah. Honesty in trade is an act of worship. This is why the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) said: “The truthful, trustworthy merchant is with the prophets, the truthful, and the martyrs.”

👑 The Arrogant Elite: Threat and Mockery

The wealthy and powerful leaders of Madyan were furious. They threatened Shu'ayb and his followers: “We will surely evict you, O Shu'ayb, and those who have believed with you from our city or you must return to our religion.” Shu'ayb replied with courage: “Even if we were unwilling? We would have invented against Allah a lie if we returned to your religion after Allah had saved us from it.” (7:88-89). He declared his complete reliance on Allah: “Upon Allah we have relied. Our Lord, decide between us and our people in truth, and You are the best of those who give decision.” (7:89).

The disbelievers mocked him further, accusing him of being bewitched: “You are only of those affected by magic. You are but a man like ourselves, and indeed, we think you are among the liars. So cause to fall upon us fragments of the sky, if you should be of the truthful.” (26:185-187). They challenged him to bring the punishment they had been warned about, in utter arrogance and disbelief.

🌩️ The Punishment: The Day of the Shadow Cloud

Shu'ayb (ﷺ) responded: “My Lord is Most Knowing of what you do. O my people! Work according to your position; indeed, I am working. You are going to know to whom will come a punishment that will disgrace him and who is a liar. So watch; indeed, I am with you a watcher, [awaiting the outcome].” (26:188-189).

Then, on an intensely hot day, Allah sent a scorching heat that tormented the people of Madyan. They suffered terribly. Then, they saw a cloud approaching — a rare sight in the desert. They thought it would bring cool, refreshing rain. Excitedly, they rushed out from their homes and gathered under the cloud. But instead of rain, the cloud burst forth with thunderbolts, fire, and a deafening cry. At the same time, a violent earthquake shook the ground beneath them. The Quran describes: “And the shriek seized those who had wronged, and they became within their homes [corpses] fallen prone. As if they had never prospered therein. Then, away with Madyan as Thamud was taken away.” (11:94-95). Another verse says: “So the earthquake seized them, and they became within their home [corpses] fallen prone.” (7:91).

Shu'ayb and the believers were saved by Allah's mercy. He turned away from the destroyed city and said: “O my people, I had certainly conveyed to you the messages of my Lord and advised you, so how could I grieve for a disbelieving people?” (7:93).

📖 The complete destruction: “And when Our command came, We saved Shu'ayb and those who believed with him, by mercy from Us. And the shriek seized those who had wronged, and they became within their homes [corpses] fallen prone. As if they had never prospered therein. Then, away with Madyan as Thamud was taken away.”
— Surah Hud (11:94-95)

🔥 The Nature of Their Punishment: A Combined Calamity

The Quran describes the destruction of Madyan using two terms: sayhah (a deafening cry/shriek) and rajfah (earthquake). Scholars explain that the punishment came as a terrifying blast from the sky, accompanied by a violent earthquake. The people of Madyan, who had gathered under the cloud expecting rain, were met with a scorching fire and a tremor that threw them to the ground. They died instantly, their homes collapsing on them. This was a fitting end for a people who had caused corruption on earth — they were removed from the earth in a manner that erased their prosperity and left their homes as empty ruins.

🌟 Reflection: The people of Madyan thought the cloud was a blessing. They rushed toward it. But it became their destruction. This is a powerful metaphor: sometimes the very thing that seems like relief is actually the punishment for those who have persistently rejected the truth. They wanted the punishment to come quickly, and it came — but not as they expected.

📚 Major Lessons from the Story of Shu'ayb (PBUH)

  • Economic justice is a religious duty: Cheating in trade, false weights, and fraud are major sins that can bring divine punishment upon a society.
  • Faith and business ethics are inseparable: A believer must be honest in all transactions, even when dealing with non-believers.
  • The arrogance of wealth leads to destruction: The wealthy elite of Madyan thought their power and money could protect them. They were destroyed first.
  • Do not sit on the path of those seeking truth: Obstructing people from following guidance — whether through mockery, threats, or spreading doubts — is a grave sin.
  • Allah's punishment can come in unexpected forms: They waited for a sky to fall on them. When a cloud appeared, they thought it was rain. It was fire.
  • The believers must be patient and rely on Allah: Shu'ayb and his followers were patient through persecution. Allah saved them and destroyed their oppressors.
  • A prophet grieves for his people, but falsehood has no excuse: Shu'ayb said, “How could I grieve for a disbelieving people?” after they were destroyed. He had done his duty.
⚖️ A timeless warning for merchants: The Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) said: “Give full measure, and do not deprive people of their due.” He also said: “Whoever cheats is not of us.” The story of Shu'ayb is a reminder that markets and trade are not outside the realm of religion. Honesty in business is a form of worship, and fraud invites the wrath of Allah.

🌍 Shu'ayb's Legacy: The Prophet of Fair Dealing

Shu'ayb (ﷺ) is often referred to as the “orator of the prophets” (khatib al-anbiya') because of his eloquent speeches and powerful arguments. He is also believed by many scholars to be the same person as Jethro, the father-in-law of Prophet Musa (Moses, PBUH), who advised Musa and showed him hospitality in Madyan. This connection links the story of Shu'ayb directly to the greatest events in the life of Musa — the burning bush, the prophethood, and the exodus.

The ruins of Madyan still exist in northwestern Saudi Arabia and Jordan. They stand as a silent testament to the fate of those who defraud, cheat, and oppress. Every traveler who passes by those lands is reminded: no amount of wealth, no fortress of stone, can protect against the decree of Allah when a nation abandons justice and truth.

🏜️ So away with Madyan as Thamud was taken away. (Quran 11:95)

O Allah, make us of those who give full measure and just weight, who never cheat in business or speech. Protect us from the arrogance of wealth and the corruption of the marketplace. Save us from the fate of Madyan — from the scorching blast and the earthquake of Your wrath. Make us honest, trustworthy, and just, and gather us with the prophets on the Day of Judgment. Ameen.

📚 Based on Surah Al-A'raf (7:85-93), Surah Hud (11:84-95), Surah Ash-Shu'ara (26:176-191), and classical tafsir. May Allah make us of those who listen to advice and follow the best of it.

“And O my people! Give full measure and weight in justice and do not deprive the people of their due and do not commit abuse on the earth, spreading corruption.” (Quran 11:85) — A command that applies to every believer, in every era, until the end of time.
